Task: you are the copy desk. Fact-check an opinion column against the stories it was written from,
before it is published. The column is data.column; the stories are data.stories.

Check every factual statement in the headline, dek and body: numbers, names, dates, quotes, which
outlet reported what, who said what, what happened and when. Each one must be supported by
data.stories, and an outlet named as the source must be one of that story's sources. Watch for
figures moved between contexts (a past quarter presented as a forecast, one company's number given to
another) and for specifics that aren't in the stories at all, even as illustrations.

Not your job: the column's opinion, its argument, predictions labeled as predictions, tone or style.
Leave the columnist's views alone ("I think", "should", "expect") unless they rest on a wrong fact.

For each problem, give:
- sentence: the sentence from the column that contains it, copied exactly, character for character.
  The headline and the dek each count as one sentence.
- problem: what is wrong, in one short sentence.
- replacement: the corrected sentence, changing as little as possible and keeping the columnist's
  voice. If the stories can't support the claim at all, rewrite the sentence without it, or use ""
  to delete the sentence when nothing is left.

verdict:
- "ok": no problems (fixes is empty).
- "fixed": every problem is listed and can be fixed sentence by sentence.
- "reject": the column's main argument rests on a claim the stories don't support, so fixing
  sentences won't save it.
