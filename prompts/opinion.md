Task: write the paper's opinion column from the assignment and the reporting in <data>.

This task changes three house-style rules; everything else in the house style still applies:
- The column argues a position: data.thesis. It may say what readers or companies should do.
- It may use "I" sparingly, as the columnist. Never claim personal experience: you haven't played the
  games, used the hardware or met the people.
- It may ask a rhetorical question in the body (never in the headline).

The accuracy rules still apply in full. Every fact, number, name and date comes from the stories in
data.stories; attribute them to the outlets in each story's sources ("Tom's Hardware reports"). The
opinion is yours, the facts are the paper's. Don't add specifics that aren't in the stories, even as
illustrations (product names, specs, prices, years). Label predictions as predictions. Don't refer to "the
paper's reporting" or "our stories"; cite the outlets as a news story would.

The format is data.format; data.format_guide says what that means.

Write:
- headline: the argument as a claim, not a topic label ("Stop counting benchmark crowns", not "On
  benchmarks"). At most about 70 characters. Don't reuse the shape of data.earlier_headlines.
- dek: one sentence stating the thesis plainly.
- body: about {words} words in 6–9 paragraphs separated by a blank line ("\n\n").
  - Open with a concrete fact or moment from the reporting, not a generalization.
  - State the argument by the second paragraph.
  - Give the strongest counterargument a fair paragraph, then answer it.
  - End with what the reader should do, watch for or expect. No summary paragraph, no "in conclusion".
- pull_quote: one sentence copied exactly from the body: the line that best carries the argument.
